Ranking Methodology

Transparent, objective, and reproducible evaluation criteria for CS research in India.

Goal & Transparency

Our goal is to provide a fully transparent, data-driven, and reproducible ranking of Indian computer science research institutions. Every score calculated in this knowledgebase is design-validated and traceable down to the specific publications, venues, and individual faculty co-authors who contributed to it.

1. Eligible Research Venues

We only consider publications in top-tier, highly-selective computer science venues. The eligible conferences are sourced directly from the CORE Rankings, specifically those rated A* and A. Journals from ACM and IEEE Transactions are also incorporated.

2. Scoring Weights

Publications are weighted according to the prestige tier of the corresponding research venue:

  • CORE A* Conference / IEEE-ACM Premium Journal: 4 points
  • CORE A Conference / IEEE-ACM Transaction: 2 points

3. Fractional Author Credit Allocation

To prevent scoring inflation on large multi-author papers, we utilize a fractional credit model. Credit for a single publication is divided equally among all its co-authors. For example:

  • A paper with 1 author receives 1.0 credit (Full weight).
  • A paper with 2 authors gives each author 0.5 credit.
  • A paper with 5 authors gives each author 0.2 credit.

4. Short, Workshop & Adjunct Proceedings Exclusions

To ensure rankings accurately reflect core, high-impact research contributions, the ingestion pipeline filters out short, non-refereed, or workshop-style papers based on the following criteria:

  • Short Papers: Publications containing 5 pages or fewer are excluded.
  • Non-core Content: Papers containing terms such as demo, poster, student abstract, doctoral consortium, extended abstract, tutorial, workshop, workshops, or companion in the title or publication venue are excluded.
  • Adjunct Proceedings: Publications in "adjunct" volumes or auxiliary proceedings of a conference (where the venue name contains the word "adjunct") are excluded.

5. Computing Institution Scores

Rather than a simple summation, an institution's final score is calculated as the geometric mean of its scores across all broad computer science research areas. This geometric mean approach rewards institutions with balanced and diverse research footprints across multiple subfields, preventing an institution from dominating the leaderboard based solely on a single high-output area.

To prevent a score of zero in any subfield from collapsing the entire geometric mean score of the institution to zero, the geometric mean is computed only over the set of areas in which the institution has a positive score (i.e., score > 0):

Institution Score = ∏a ∈ Apos (Sa)1 / |Apos|
Where:
  • Apos is the set of all research areas in which the institution has a score strictly greater than zero (Sa > 0).
  • Sa is the total weighted score of the institution in research area a, computed by summing credit × weight for all eligible publication authorships in that area.
  • If an institution has no publications (|Apos| = 0), its score is 0.0.

6. Primary Data Sources

The data pipelines verify publications through three core channels:

  • IRINS: Used for mapping faculty profiles to their Indian parent institution.
  • DBLP: Used as the gold-standard publication repository to verify titles and authorship metadata.
  • CORE: Used to lookup quality ratings of conferences.
